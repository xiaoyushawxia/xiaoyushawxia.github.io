Visitors

    I am a Senior Lecturer (equivalent to Associate Professor in North America) in Cyber Security & Software Systems with the School of Computing Technologies at RMIT University. My research asks what large-scale AI systems remember — and how that memory can be audited and revoked at a cost that holds from the datacentre down to the device. My current interests include Machine Unlearning and Model Memorization, Privacy and Security of LLM Serving Systems (KV and semantic caching, retrieval augmentation), and Agent Memory, Provenance, and Accountability across federated, edge, and on-device deployments.

    I am a Senior Member of IEEE and a Member of ACM. I serve as an Associate Editor for IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing (TDSC), IEEE Transactions on Mobile Computing (TMC) and IEEE Transactions on Services Computing (TSC), and as a Review Board Member of IEEE Transactions on Parallel and Distributed Systems (TPDS). I co-organize workshops and special sessions on the privacy and security of AI systems, including ACM LAMPS (co-located with CCS), ACM CDM and an IJCNN special session on generative models, as well as the IEEE World Congress on SERVICES.

    I am actively looking for self-motivated Ph.D. students interested in machine unlearning, the privacy and security of LLM serving systems, and on-device and edge LLMs.

    Each year I usually take on one or two new PhD students on project-funded or RTP scholarships. For these positions, I would love to hear from applicants with:

    1. A solid computer science background, with a GPA of 3.5/4.0 or above (or equivalent).
    2. At least one first-author paper in a CORE A*/A or CCF-A conference or journal.
    3. IELTS 6.5 overall, with no band below 6.0, or equivalent.
    4. Preferred (not required): experience in security or privacy research, systems engineering, CTF or vulnerability research, or reproducing published attacks.

    If you are interested, please email me your CV, transcript and research experience, with a short paragraph on any security or privacy paper you found interesting (it need not be one of mine) and how you would attack it. I may not reply to emails that read as entirely AI-generated.

    Visiting scholars and students, industry partners, domestic students and applicants with external scholarships are all welcome; the requirements above do not apply in these cases.
